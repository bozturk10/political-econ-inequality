# CCT and government timeline: coding and sources

The chart is a descriptive alignment of presidential terms and program names, 1997–2026. Its bars are **not** estimates of any government's effect on inequality or of CCT treatment effects. The right edge is the chart boundary.

**Term sources:** [Brazil Casa Civil, presidential archives](https://www.gov.br/casacivil/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/receitas-e-despesas/acervos-de-mandatos-presidenciais-encerrados); [Mexico INE, electoral alternation and presidential periods](https://www.ine.mx/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/20AlternanciaElectoralNoresteMexico.pdf); [Mexico's official Sheinbaum term decree](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5737865).

**Ideology coding:** These four labels summarize party families and government positions. They are interpretive, particularly for Mexico's heterogeneous PRI and Brazil's coalition governments; they are not a one-dimensional measured score that is directly comparable across countries. Sources: [Brazilian party and presidential ideology research](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-politics-and-society/article/ideology-of-brazilian-parties-and-presidents-a-research-note-on-coalitional-presidentialism-under-stress/FAF5489B3E01D39A16DBB4971458AD48); [Wilson Center on Mexico's PRI, PAN and Morena](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/microsite/3/node/116853); [International IDEA on Morena](https://www.idea.int/publications/catalogue/html/informe-sobre-las-elecciones-de-2024-en-mexico-la-continuidad-del). PRI is plotted as centre/mixed rather than uniformly right. PSDB and MDB are represented by the broad centre-right category for the administrations shown; those labels can be contested.

## Programs and interpretation

- Mexico: Progresa began in 1997, was renamed Oportunidades in 2002 and Prospera in 2014. After 2019, Benito Juárez scholarships changed the institutional design, so the chart uses an outlined bar rather than implying an unchanged CCT. [World Bank program history](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/154251517454030254/pdf/Mexico-PP-01092018.pdf); [CONEVAL monitoring](https://www.coneval.org.mx/InformesPublicaciones/Documents/FIMyE_19-20.pdf).
- Brazil: Bolsa Família was created in 2003 from earlier transfers, replaced by Auxílio Brasil in late 2021, and restored in 2023. [2003 founding measure](https://legislacao.presidencia.gov.br/atos/?ano=2003&ato=33dgXUU50dRpWTa36&numero=132&tipo=MPV); [Brazil MDS program history](https://www.gov.br/mds/pt-br/noticias-e-conteudos/desenvolvimento-social/noticias-desenvolvimento-social/bolsa-familia-reforca-combate-a-pobreza-e-melhora-seguranca-alimentar-aponta-estudo/40_Caderno_Estudos.pdf).
- The presentation's selected program-era windows are Mexico 1997–2014 and Brazil 2003–2014, marked separately on the timeline. These are research-design periods, not an assertion that any household-survey series is strictly comparable across every year. Mexico's ENIGH series has methodological breaks at 2006–2008 and 2014–2016. See [comparison_window_decision.md](../docs/comparison_window_decision.md) and the [World Bank's Mexico Poverty and Equity Assessment](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099021925181518988/pdf/P179488-c49ff360-e38a-4961-85e2-1ef4e6cc5294.pdf).

Government and program intervals sometimes cut across exact launch months; the chart's year-level program endpoints are approximate. A program's continuation across an election does not by itself establish policy continuity in coverage, benefit size, conditions, or administration.
